# Changelog

## Unreleased
- Treatment kinds (F20, data overview clean-up asked for by Richard 2026-10-18): every treatment label is sorted into drug or compound, genetic background, condition change, culture format, virus/bacteria/cells, or unclassified by ordered word rules in `treatment_classes.yaml` (`treatment_class.py`). A name on a "Drug Exposure" screen counts as a compound unless a rule says otherwise (3D growth, hypoxia...); a name no rule fits stays "unclassified", never guessed. The site export gets a new screen field `treatment_category` and `by_treatment_category` counts in `summary.json`; the lookup report lists every non-drug label and everything unclassified so the rules can be tightened. No change to the unified data model, only a derived export field. The overview page itself is built by the redesign thread.
- Drug identifier lookup, cleaner names: bracketed doses ("THZ-1(50nmol/L)") are dropped, bracketed aliases ("NU 7441 (KU-57788)") are tried when the main name has no match, viruses, bacteria and cells are tagged "not_a_compound" and not looked up, and the report is printed in the run log. First real run: 317 names, 158 labels matched, 5 ambiguous, 166 unmatched, 30 background only.
- Redesign step 4a (F22): `/drug` has the explorer layout. Drug, drug screen, no-drug screen, highlight gene and a colour gap sit in a left panel; the 640 px canvas is replaced by a large Plotly scatter (WebGL where available) with axes named after each screen's score, a dashed "no change" diagonal, genes coloured when their drug score differs from the no-drug score by a chosen gap in robust-z units (default 3, a reading aid, not a test), and click-to-highlight. The two top-difference tables sit side by side and link to `/gene`. Data, scores and statistics are unchanged. A precomputed per-drug view of consistently shifted genes is not part of this PR; it needs Richard's approval of the method first.
- Redesign step 3b (F23): new page `/associations` shows the precomputed associations: pick a question (a tissue, or a driver-gene mutation), set the false discovery rate, minimum effect and direction at the left, read a Plotly volcano plot (effect against evidence for every gene tested; click a dot to open the gene) and the table of the strongest genes, and find any gene by symbol. A plain-language "How these numbers were made" box with the limits (tissue and mutation are tangled, questions are corrected one at a time) is on the page, as Richard asked. Appears empty ("Not published yet") until the Associations workflow has been run with "publish" ticked. No new data, dependency or statistics.
- Drug identifier lookup is faster and visible: 4 parallel workers behind one shared rate limit (4 calls per second in total), progress lines every 20 names, 4xx answers treated as "no match", failed calls counted in the report instead of crashing the run. The first run (about 30 minutes, no output) was cancelled.
- Redesign step 3a, precomputed associations pipeline (F23 groundwork, method approved by Richard 2026-10-18): `datasets/associations/build.py` runs the biological-workflow comparison for every gene against each tissue (vs all others) and each driver-panel gene mutation (vs wild-type), with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR per question (the only new statistical element). Output `assoc/index.json` and one file per question. Unit tests on synthetic data, SciPy reference tests (`tests/reference/test_associations_reference.py`), and a workflow "Associations" that builds from the staging data, fails unless KRAS, BRAF, PIK3CA and TP53/MDM2 come out right, and can upload to R2 staging by hand. The volcano page follows in step 3b.
- Redesign step 2 (F20, F21): `/gene` is now the "Gene explorer", laid out like the DepMap explorer. Controls sit in a left panel (gene, Reactome pathway, filters, which the table now renders there via `filtersIn`); the main area has a Plotly box-and-dots chart of the gene's rank in every screen, grouped by tissue, cell line, treatment or phenotype (`src/scripts/explorer.ts`, rarest groups pooled into "other" rather than dropped), then the biological-workflow comparison (also Plotly) and the results table with its detail panel. Clicking a dot selects its table row. No new data, dependency or statistics.
- Redesign step 1 (owner request 2026-10-18): the Victorian look is dropped for a clean, neutral data-explorer UI. New tokens (light and dark, colour-blind-safe chart colours), system font stack (web fonts and watercolour icons removed), compact top bar, rewritten stylesheet and home page, new style guide. Plotly (`plotly.js-cartesian-dist-min`, MIT) is added as a runtime dependency, listed in the spec; `src/scripts/charts.ts` loads it on demand with token colours. `/data` overview now uses Plotly bar charts. Workflow `update-lockfile.yml` regenerates `package-lock.json` on a runner when a PR is labelled `update-lockfile`, because the sandbox cannot reach the npm registry. Next steps: gene explorer with Plotly, precomputed associations, drug view.
- M3 copy-number artefact check and paralog check, same approved method (no new statistics): new split "a gene gaining copies" (gain or amplified against normal copy number) in `grouping.ts`, so a dependency on an amplified gene can be told apart from a gene that is merely dosage-sensitive. `/gene` lists four textbook examples (ERBB2 by ERBB2 gain, SMARCA2 by SMARCA4 off, ARID1B by ARID1A mutation, PRMT5 by MTAP loss) that open with the form filled in and the comparison run (`?g=X&by=...&y=Y`). The live validator `validate_recipe_live.mjs` now covers all split types and these four cases (run it by hand via "Validate recipes"). A per-screen copy-number correlation would be a new method and is not built.
- F20 drug identifiers, step 1 (Richard approved PubChem and ChEMBL as sources, 2026-10-18): `drug_names.py` splits each treatment label into genetic background and drug components and looks each drug up by name in PubChem (CID) and ChEMBL (ID, exact preferred name only); `fetch_drugs.py` and the manual workflow "Draft drug identifiers" run it on a GitHub runner and attach `drugs.draft.yaml` plus a report of unmapped or ambiguous names. Nothing is published and the site does not use it yet; a person reviews the draft before it becomes `drugs.yaml`.
- Wording (Richard, 2026-10-18): "recipes" are now called "biological workflows" on the site, in code (`src/scripts/workflow.ts`, `tests/workflow.spec.ts`, `tests/reference/*workflow*`) and in the plan. Still named "recipe" on purpose, because they need Richard's approval to change: `AGENTS.md`, `docs/spec.md`, and the file `.github/workflows/validate-recipes.yml` with the script it calls (`tests/reference/validate_recipe_live.mjs`). Older changelog entries keep the old word.
- M3 recipes 3 to 7, same approved method: the comparison on `/gene` can now split cell lines by a gene's mutation, by a gene being switched off (log2 TPM+1 below 1) against expressed (3 or above), by a gene losing copies against normal, by whole-genome doubling, or by tissue against all others (`src/scripts/grouping.ts`). Lines in neither group and flagged lines are left out.
- F22 gene page: "Response to drugs" table on `/gene` (per treatment: screens, cell lines, source-called hits, each linking to `/drug?d=`); `/drug` preselects the drug from `?d=`. Counts only, because drug-screen scores are not comparable across papers.
- Reactome pathway picker (F20, requested by Richard 2026-10-18): `datasets/pathways/build.py` downloads Reactome's human pathway file (GMT, standard library only), keeps valid pathways, writes rejected lines with reasons and records the licence page text in `report.md`; new workflow "Reactome pathways" (tests and build on PRs, optional upload to R2 staging by hand). `/gene` gets a "Browse by pathway" box that limits the gene dropdown to a pathway's genes, lists them as buttons, and shows the most specific pathways of the looked-up gene. Appears only once the file is published. Licence CC BY 4.0, confirmed by Richard 2026-10-18; the credit is shown under the pathway box. Needs one run of the workflow with "publish" ticked.
- Desktop-first workspace (F4, F11, F20, F21): `/data` and `/gene` use the full window width on wide screens with a sticky filter panel at the left, a scrolling table with sticky header and first column, and a detail panel at the right (below 90rem it sits under the table; below 64rem everything stacks as before). Click a row or use the arrow keys to select it; press `/` to jump to search. On `/gene` the rank plot follows the filters, hovering a dot names its screen, and clicking a dot selects its row. Gene lookup now has a dropdown of known symbols (`src/scripts/combobox.ts`) and rejects unknown symbols with suggestions; the same dropdown is on the "mutation in" gene field. No new dependencies or data. Not yet done: Reactome pathway filters (needs a data source and licence decision).
- F22 drug page (`/drug`): pick a drug, one of its screens and a matched no-drug screen (match level shown), see a scatter of gene scores without and with the drug, highlight a gene, and read the genes with the largest difference. Scores are each screen's native primary score as reported, as a within-screen robust z (no depleted/enriched alignment; decision of Richard 2026-10-18); no new statistics. `export_site.py` now also writes `perts.json` and `s/<NNNN>.json` per plottable screen (about 800 files; duplicate gene rows are folded to their median and counted in `summary.json`), and `screens.json` gains `primary_score_name`, `primary_score_position`, `has_z`. Controls must have a signed primary score. Needs one re-run of "Publish data to R2 staging"; the Parquet-reading part runs for the first time there.
- F22 groundwork for the drug page: `pairing.py` links each drug screen to untreated, full-genome screens of the same cell line, labelled by match level (same study and library, same study, or another study on the same cell line; at most 3 of the last kind). Unmatched drug screens are kept with match "none". `export_site.py` writes `drug_pairs.json` and adds counts to `summary.json`. On the ORCS 2.0.18 index: 484 drug screens, best match same study and library 63, same cell line only 348, none 73.

- M3 recipe 2 (method approved by Richard 2026-10-11): on `/gene`, "Does it depend on a mutation?" compares the gene's essential-scale values between lines with and without a mutation in a second gene. One value per line (median of its screens), difference of medians with a seeded bootstrap 95% interval, two-sided Mann-Whitney test, at least 5 lines per group; flagged lines and lines without mutation calls are left out (`src/scripts/recipe.ts`). Independent SciPy reference test in CI; manual "Validate recipes" workflow checks known biology (KRAS, BRAF, PIK3CA, TP53, MDM2) on staging data. `lines.json` gains `mutation_calls`.
